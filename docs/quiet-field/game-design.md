# Quiet Field 2: game design proposal, "The Night Before"

## 1. Why it's boring today
- **The world never changes.** Every screen is the same cream card on a static backdrop. The "field" is a 4×3 grid of grey emoji tiles, and lighting a lantern turns one tile yellow. Hall of Presidents had doors that opened, wings that unlocked and parades. Quiet Field has a toast.
- **She never gets to be right.** Echo and Walk end on "I said it", which is an honour button. Being right is what drives Sarah most, and nothing here confirms it. The stars (✨14) buy nothing.
- **All or nothing.** The whole line blurs as one block. Lanterns run from 10 to 46 words, and one Peek shows everything.
- **The hard parts are never practised.** Recitals break at the joins between lines ("…can long endure. / We are met…") and on near-duplicates: *here* appears 8 times, and the speech has both "dedicated here" and "here dedicated". Mix is a 50/50 guess between two buttons.
- **Thin feedback.** A toast plus a counter. No sound effects, no motion, and a miss gets "Try the other way."
- **Generic visuals.** Emoji (✓, 🌍, 🗽) instead of Hall's illustrated cards. The 🗽 is also wrong for 1863: the statue came in 1886.
- **No destination.** Six buttons plus a nav bar that repeats them, and no ending beyond one line of text.

## 2. Core concept: *The Night Before*
On **18 November 1863** Lincoln arrives by train at dusk in a town overflowing with visitors. He finishes the speech upstairs in the Wills House and delivers it the next afternoon on Cemetery Hill.

**Sarah walks that evening.**
- **The path.** A side-scrolling SVG path runs **from the railway depot, through the Diamond (the town square), up Baltimore Street, to the cemetery gatehouse and the speakers' platform**. It has 12 lantern posts, and they keep the ids p1–p12, so no progress is lost.
- **Wicks.** Each lantern holds 1–3 **wicks**: clauses of 8–16 words, cut where a speaker breathes. That's about 20 wicks (p1, p4 and p9 get 2 each; p12 gets 3). This fixes the uneven cuts.
- **A growing crowd.** Every solid wick adds silhouettes to the path: soldiers, families, a reporter, a boy on a fence. They stay, and they are hers.
- **The light changes once.** Two weeks of dusk, then **the Dedication** in pale November daylight. It is the first palette change, and that change *is* the payoff.

Tone: a museum at closing time. Quiet, warm, true, never cartoonish.

## 3. Practice games
- **Only produced recall counts.** Letter Trail, Night Walk and Echo with the mic can raise a wick's state, and "solid" needs success on two separate days. The recognition games are for variety only.
- **Every miss looks the same.** The right word writes itself in **soft gold ink**, the lantern flickers but never goes out, and play continues.
- **Two misses on one wick:** the voice says the wick warmly, and it is "set by the window for tomorrow."
- **Tricky words.** Missed words go into a private list that Seams and Proof draw from first.

1. **Echo** (hearing and rhythm). She hears one wick with karaoke highlighting from word timestamps, then says it back (mic optional). *Juice:* recognised words light up in sequence, then the wick ignites.
2. **Letter Trail** (cued recall, then free recall; the core game). Only first letters show (*F s a s y a…*). She types the next letter and the whole word inks in. *Juice:* a pentatonic tick that rises with each word, a flame jump on every run of 5, and an ignition chord at the end. "Spicy, by choice" hides the letters, so she types whole words.
3. **Fading Ink** (gradual deletion). She reads the wick aloud and 2–3 words fade each pass, small words first. When the page is blank, a Letter Trail check follows. *Juice:* the words dissolve into embers that rise into the lantern.
4. **The Seams** (joins between lines). She sees the end of one wick and gives the first three words of the next. *Juice:* a spark arcs post to post, and a chain of seams strings lights along the path.
5. **Printer's Proof** (look-alike wording). A newspaper proof has one wrong word ("The world will little *notice*") and she strikes it out with a quill. This echoes the garbled 1863 newspaper printings. *Juice:* the type clacks into place, and each caught word goes into a "pied type" tray she collects.
6. **Scattered Pages** (order). Wind scatters 4–6 wick cards and she drags them onto a string between posts. *Juice:* a click per card, and the string glows when complete. A card in the wrong place just floats back.
7. **Night Walk** (the whole speech). She recites from post 1 to the last lit post with only the pictures showing. Tap a picture once for first letters, again for the words. *Juice:* footsteps, lanterns brightening, the crowd turning to watch.

## 4. Progression, sessions, hooks, ceremony
**Session (8–10 min), started from one "Tonight" button:**
1. **Arrival:** something changed overnight (a new figure, or an object glinting on the path).
2. **Night Walk warm-up** (2 min).
3. **New wick:** Echo, then Fading Ink, then Letter Trail (3 min).
4. **One rotating game** (2 min).
5. **Lighting:** a bell tolls if a lantern became solid.
6. **Close:** a candle in the Wills House window and a teaser ("Tomorrow: serenaders under his window").

She can play up to two "one more" rounds, and they are games, not drill.

**Arc** (adapts to her pace):

| Days | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Depot to the Diamond | Lanterns 1–4 |
| 4–7 | Baltimore Street | Lanterns 5–8, plus the join across the paragraph break |
| 8–10 | The hill | Lanterns 9–12, with extra Fading Ink on p12 |
| 11–13 | The whole path | Full walks, Proofs on tricky words, Seams chains; rehearsals open |
| When all wicks are solid | The platform | **The Dedication** |

**Hooks for a 2e collector:**
- **Field Notes.** One true primary-source card per lantern: the 87-year arithmetic, Everett's two-hour oration, Everett's letter ("…as near to the central idea… in two hours as you did in two minutes"), and the one blurry photo of Lincoln on the platform. Cards not yet found show as silhouettes.
- **The Five Copies** (the long mystery). Five manuscripts exist: Nicolay, Hay, Everett, Bancroft and Bliss. Each band of lanterns unlocks one, and she finds a real difference from the Bliss text she is learning (the Nicolay copy has no "under God").
- **Word Detective.** Which word appears 8 times? (*here*.) How many forms of *dedicate*? (6.) Each answer doubles as a memory hook.

**The ceremony, without perfectionist pressure:**
- **Rehearsal** is always private and allows hints. It is never recorded or scored.
- **The Dedication is a scene, not a test.** Her own crowd, a hymn chorale, "Mr. Everett has spoken for two hours…", then her turn.
- **She chooses how to deliver it:** *With my page* (a first-letter cue sheet; Lincoln read from his pages too) or *From memory*. Both complete it. Hint taps are allowed and never shown.
- **No score.** A certificate reads "Delivered by Sarah" with the date. The applause plays whatever happens, then Everett's letter arrives as a Field Note.
- **Optional recording.** MediaRecorder, saved to IndexedDB on the device only. She can play it back, keep it, or let it go. "Hold another dedication" is always available, and her certificates stay.
- **Invite a listener (Dad).** This is the real payoff.

## 5. Visual and sound direction
**Colour tokens.** Keep `--dusk`, `--navy`, `--lamp`, `--glow`, `--paper` and `--ink`. Add `--ember #c47a4a`, `--brick #6e3b2e` and `--shadow #121826`. Add `--frost #c9d3dc` and `--noon #e9e4d6` for the Dedication only.

**Scene.** One inline SVG in three parallax layers moved with `transform`:
- the ridge
- the town: lit windows, the twin-arch gatehouse, the Wills House
- the path with its posts

**Lanterns.**
- A flame path plus a `radialGradient` glow with `mix-blend-mode:screen`.
- `@keyframes flicker` on scale and opacity, with a random 2–3 s duration and delay per post.
- Ignition: a 400 ms scale-up, a glow ring, and 6–10 ember circles rising over 1.2 s.

**Crowd.** 5–6 `<symbol>` silhouettes (top hat, bonnet, kepi, child, man on crutches) placed with `<use>`, rim-lit toward the nearest lit post.

**Type.** The speech is set in a serif (Iowan Old Style, falling back to Georgia) as ink on paper. Recalled words turn `--lamp` with a soft glow.

**Pictures.** Two-tone SVG pictures true to 1863 replace the emoji. For p12: three doors under one lamp.

**Reduced motion.** `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off the parallax and embers.

**Sound** (WebAudio; the voice is the only audio file):
- faint low-passed wind
- a crowd murmur that grows with the crowd
- a triangle tick per word, climbing a pentatonic scale
- a filtered-noise "whoomp" plus a warm sine sixth when a wick ignites
- one distant bell (inharmonic partials) when a lantern turns solid
- silence on a miss
- a sine-pad chorale for the ceremony

The "imagined Lincoln" voice, with word timestamps, drives the karaoke, Fading Ink and exact playback of each wick.

## 6. Build priorities
**Tonight (/field-next/):**
1. **Wick data.** Add `wicks:[…]` to each lantern, keep the p1–p12 ids and states, and roll wick progress up into the lantern state.
2. **Letter Trail.** Include the gold-ink misses, the two-miss "tomorrow" rule, and the tick, ignition and bell sounds. This is the biggest gain in both fun and honest evidence of recall.
3. **Path landscape** to replace the tile grid: flickering posts, the "Tonight" button, and the code that runs a session.
4. **The Seams.** Cheap to build, high payoff.

**Next 2–4 days:** Fading Ink, Night Walk, the crowd, Field Notes, and the two-day rule for "solid".

**Before her day ~11:** the Dedication, with the page/memory choice, recording and certificate.

**After that:** Proof, Pages, the Five Copies, the new voice and the new pictures.

**Don't touch:** the household, profile and sync code. The Preamble can reuse the wick engine later.
